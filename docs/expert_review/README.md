# Prospective blind expert review

No expert ratings have been collected. These packets use genuine frozen 2023/24 FBref profiles. They compare each of the six style affinity shortlists with a random eligible-player baseline. This is a first human validation protocol, not evidence that the system recommends successful transfers.

Before recruitment, agree the six style briefs, scoring anchors and minimum exposure with at least two independent football scouts. The present broad briefs contain no club-specific budget claim; a later club study must preregister its requirements. Use the same information for A/B, randomize presentation order, hide affinity scores and algorithm labels, and provide video/context consistently if available. Player names remain visible, so reputation and retrospective knowledge may bias judgments. Ask experts to rate the statistical snapshot rather than later careers. Analysts holding the method key must not act as blinded reviewers.

Generate packets: `python -m scripts.expert_review_packets`. Share `blinded_shortlists.csv` and `rating_template.csv`, not `data/expert_review/private/method_key.csv`. Enter an agreed alias, tactical fit 1–5 (1 unsuitable, 3 plausible, 5 strong), shortlist judgment `yes`/`no`, confidence 1–5 and a written reason. Store completed forms under ignored `data/expert_review/private/ratings.csv`. Obtain participant consent and avoid personal contact details in forms.

Run `notebooks/expert_validation.ipynb` after collection. Primary comparison is paired mean tactical-fit difference per expert and brief; secondary measure is shortlist precision at reviewed K. Report number of independent experts and briefs, disagreement and qualitative reasons. Do not treat player rows as independent expert samples or claim predictive transfer success. Current analysis deliberately returns pending when forms are blank. Larger preregistered studies should add expert-cluster bootstrap intervals and inter-rater agreement once sufficient matched reviews exist.
